Slider: drag it from 240 min (far left) to 15 min (far right); the orange UST line moves towards the theoretical production and the summary table below follows. It only switches the lead time, nothing is reloaded. Lead time = how many minutes before the interval starts the forecast was issued. Each issue is available about 26 minutes after its issue time and intraday gates close roughly 15 minutes before delivery, so 45 min is the shortest lead time that can still be used for trading (default 60 min); 15 and 30 min are only meaningful for after-the-fact evaluation.
Heat map: one row per lead time (plus the DA forecast on top), one column per interval of the selected period (nights without forecast are left out). The colour is the error = forecast − theoretical production in kWh per interval: red = forecast too high, blue = too low, white = close. The row of the selected lead time has a light-purple frame. Hover a cell for the forecast, the theoretical value and the error; scroll or drag the slider under the line chart to zoom (both charts zoom together). Each cell uses its own lead time only, there is no fallback to another lead time.
Production forecast (DA) (grey dashed line) is the supplier’s day-ahead POA forecast converted to energy with our own formula (DC capacity × monthly PR, capped at the AC limit), the same formula as the theoretical production and the ultra-short-term forecast.
Ultra-short-term (UST) forecast is the supplier’s rolling POA forecast (a new issue every 15 minutes, 0–4 h ahead), converted with the same formula. It is a shadow forecast, not a declaration basis. The line is made of three sources with the same format: the supplier’s backtest (hindcast, 2026-01-01 to 2026-10-25), their after-the-fact replay (from 2026-10-25) and the live shadow run (from 2026-10-29). Where several exist for a cell the live value wins, otherwise replay, otherwise hindcast. Backtest and replay assume the newest measurement at issue time and look better than what live achieves while its measurement input is stale. In the All sites view an interval counts only if all five sites have a value. In hourly view four 15-min values are summed (all four must exist).

Summary by site

Selected period · UST at the lead time chosen above

Theoretical production is calculated from VCOM POA irradiance (includes the AC limit, excludes grid curtailment); it is not the meter reading. Statistics only use intervals where theoretical production, DA and the UST forecast are all available, so both forecasts are compared on the same intervals.
Dev. % = (Σforecast − Σtheoretical) / Σtheoretical — net bias; over- and under-forecasts cancel out (negative = forecast too low overall).
Abs. error % = Σ|forecast − theoretical| / Σtheoretical — every interval’s error counts in full, so errors do not cancel out.

One forecast issue · accuracy by lead time

kWh per interval · Budapest local time · one day, uses the site buttons above

Issue time is the moment a forecast was issued; that single issue forecasts the next 16 intervals (15 to 240 min ahead, shown as the orange segment with a dotted line at the issue time). Drag the slider to move the issue time through the day: the points right after the issue time sit on the theoretical production, the later ones drift away. Bars: absolute error of that issue at each lead time; dashed line: average absolute error at that lead time over all issue times of the day. First hour = leads 15–60 min, last hour = leads 195–240 min; abs. error % = Σ|forecast − theoretical| / Σtheoretical over those intervals, and DA is compared on the same 4 hours. The default day is yesterday because the theoretical production of today is only complete in the evening. Backtest and replay days look better than live while the live measurement input is stale.
